Water Treatment Risk Hierarchy

The audit is built top-down from what threatens people in this industry.

This hierarchy is drawn from OSHA and NIOSH fatality investigations and documented industry hazard data for water and wastewater operations. Every hazard — and every finding the audit produces — is ranked on five factors: severity, likelihood, regulatory exposure, operational impact, and business impact, with severity leading the sort. Audit depth follows the ranking: Tier 1 hazards get the deepest field verification — atmosphere testing procedures, rescue readiness, energy isolation — while frequent lower-severity exposures are engineered into daily habits through the culture program.

Tier 1 · Catastrophic — fatality potential Tier 2 · Critical — life-altering injury Tier 3 · Serious — lost time & chronic illness Tier 4 · Frequent — highest likelihood day-to-day

Ranking factors:  Severity  ·  Likelihood  ·  Regulatory Exposure  ·  Operational Impact  ·  Business Impact

Tier 1 — CatastrophicSingle-event fatality / multiple-fatality potential
1
Confined Space + Toxic Atmosphere (H2S · Methane · O2 Deficiency)

The documented #1 killer in water and wastewater work. H2S paralyzes the sense of smell above 100 ppm and drops workers in seconds above 500 ppm. Most fatal events multiply — untrained would-be rescuers follow the first victim in (the "rescue cascade" OSHA and the CSB have warned about for decades).

How the audit attacks it

Permit program torn down line by line: atmosphere testing practice (not just the written program), monitor calibration records, ventilation, attendant duties, and a live rescue-plan walkthrough at every entry point — tanks, wet wells, digesters, vessels. Gas detection is Canon's home turf.

2
Treatment Chemical Gas Release (Chlorine · Chlorine Dioxide · Ozone · Ammonia)

Disinfection chemicals carry mass-exposure potential for workers and neighbors alike — the reason EPA RMP and OSHA PSM thresholds exist for these inventories.

How the audit attacks it

Chemical inventory vs. RMP/PSM thresholds, storage and changeout practices, leak detection and alarm function tests, emergency response drills, and SDS-to-reality verification on the floor.

3
Engulfment & Drowning

Wet wells, clarifiers, recirculation pits, and open basins — a fall into moving water or sludge in a confined structure is rarely survivable.

How the audit attacks it

Guarding and grating integrity walkdown, fall-into-water controls, retrieval equipment placement, and lone-worker rules for tank-top and basin work.

4
Hazardous Energy — LOTO & Machine Entanglement

Pumps, screens, augers, conveyors, and presses restart without warning when isolation fails. Fabrication shops add the full machine-guarding exposure on top.

How the audit attacks it

Live lockout audits on real equipment with the technicians who do it — device by device — plus machine guarding verification across shop and plant floor.

5
Electrical & Arc Flash in Wet Environments

High-voltage MCCs, pump panels, and standing water share the same rooms in treatment facilities. Arc flash energy at these panels is life-ending.

How the audit attacks it

Arc flash labeling and study currency, qualified-person verification, panel clearances, GFCI protection, and wet-location wiring condition.

6
Fire & Explosion (Digester Methane · Hot Work)

Anaerobic digestion produces methane; welding and cutting happen in fabrication and maintenance daily. The two must never meet unmanaged.

How the audit attacks it

Hot work permit practice, combustible atmosphere testing before torch work, digester gas system integrity, and flammable storage walkdown.

7
Vehicle & Driving Exposure — Field Crews

Mobile service is a driving business, and roadway crashes remain a leading cause of workplace death across every industry. Exposure scales with every service call.

How the audit attacks it

DOT/FMCSA file audit, hours-of-service and inspection practice, and a telematics-based driving scorecard — a program Canon already runs live for heavy industrial fleets.

Tier 2 — CriticalHospitalization / life-altering injury
8
Falls From Height

Tank tops, elevated platforms, fixed ladders, and truck/trailer access — the classic serious-injury generator in plants and shops.

How the audit attacks it

Anchor points, guardrail and ladder condition, harness inspection records, and how work-at-height actually gets done versus how the program says it does.

9
Chemical Contact — Burns & Splashes

Acids, caustics, hypochlorite, ferric chloride, and polymers are handled at transfer points, day tanks, and media changeouts.

How the audit attacks it

Transfer and changeout procedures observed live, PPE matched to SDS requirements, eyewash/shower placement and test records.

10
Struck-By / Caught-Between — Lifting & Rigging

Vessels, skids, and filter housings are heavy; cranes, hoists, and forklifts move them daily through fabrication and installs.

How the audit attacks it

Rigging gear inspection, operator certification and evaluation currency, load path discipline, and forklift behavior on the floor.

11
Trenching & Excavation — Install Crews

Field installation of treatment systems and piping puts crews in and around excavations on customer sites — so Canon audits where the work happens.

How the audit attacks it

Competent-person designation, protective system practice, and utility locate discipline verified on live field jobs.

E1
Uncontrolled Environmental Release — Spills, Exceedances & Permit Violations

Ranked here on regulatory exposure and business impact rather than injury severity: a reportable spill, discharge exceedance, or lapsed permit carries enforcement, fines, and reputational cost for the whole platform — and in a water business, environmental performance is the product.

How the audit attacks it

Permit inventory per site with expirations and enforcement history, discharge sampling and reporting practice, SPCC and secondary containment walkdown, hazardous waste determinations and manifests, and spill response readiness verified where chemicals are actually transferred and stored. Full scope in Framework Section 10.

Tier 3 — SeriousLost-time injury & chronic occupational illness
12
Pathogen & Biological Exposure

Raw wastewater carries hepatitis, bacteria, and bloodborne hazards — a constant low-visibility exposure for operators and service techs.

How the audit attacks it

Hygiene facilities and practice, vaccination program review, PPE and decon procedures, exposure control plan reality-check.

13
Respirable Dust & Welding Fume (Silica · Hexavalent Chrome · Carbon Dust)

Multimedia filter work means sand, garnet, anthracite, and activated carbon; stainless fabrication means hex chrome fume. Chronic, compensable, and invisible.

How the audit attacks it

Exposure screening walkdown; where sampling is warranted, certified industrial hygiene professionals are scoped in for dosimetry and air quality — practical first, specialists where they earn their keep.

14
Noise-Induced Hearing Loss

Blowers, pumps, compressors, and grinding — hearing loss is permanent and among the most common occupational illnesses in treatment and fabrication.

How the audit attacks it

Hearing conservation program review, audiometric testing currency, and IH dosimetry scoped where screening flags it.

15
Heat Stress — Outdoor & Field Work

Texas and Gulf-region service work puts techs in extreme heat with task-driven schedules.

How the audit attacks it

Water/rest/shade practice on live jobs, acclimatization for new hires, and heat metrics in the daily field routine.

Tier 4 — FrequentHighest day-to-day likelihood — where recordables actually come from
16
Slips, Trips & Same-Level Falls

Wet floors are the permanent condition of a water business. Lower severity per event — but the single most frequent injury source in the industry.

How the audit attacks it

Housekeeping and drainage walkdown, footwear standards, and — the real fix — daily peer observations that make wet-surface awareness a habit, not a poster.

17
Manual Handling & Ergonomics

Media bags, filter elements, hose runs, and valve work generate sprains and strains — the quiet driver of workers' comp cost.

How the audit attacks it

Task observation on the heaviest recurring jobs, mechanical-assist opportunities, and team-lift norms built into the observation program.

18
Hand Injuries & Lacerations

Fabrication, media handling, and maintenance put hands on sharp, hot, and pinching surfaces all day.

How the audit attacks it

Glove standard vs. task mapping, sharp-edge controls in fabrication, and hand-placement as a standing observation topic.

Hierarchy sources: OSHA hydrogen sulfide guidance and confined-space enforcement history, NIOSH/CDC fatality investigation reports for wastewater facilities, U.S. Chemical Safety Board findings on toxic-atmosphere rescue cascades, and published industry hazard analyses for water/wastewater operations. Rankings follow a consistent five-factor methodology — severity, likelihood, regulatory exposure, operational impact, and business impact — with single-event severity leading the sort; environmental entries (E-series) rank on regulatory exposure and business impact. Every finding written during the audit is prioritized on the same five factors, so the matrix and the findings tracker speak one language. The matrix is re-scored per site during Phase 1 — each facility gets its own ranked profile in the hub.

The Audit Framework

Section by section — the same structure your program will live in.

This framework follows the field-proven contractor safety-program format used across heavy industry — the structure insurers and operators already recognize — translated to water treatment operations, and extended with a dedicated environmental stewardship chapter, because in a water business environmental performance is operational performance. Each section below is an audit chapter in Phase 1, a findings category in the hub during Phase 2, and the skeleton of the written IWS EHS program going forward. Audit one document structure; inherit a working program.

1Commitment to Safety — Leadership Policy▸
The Program Element

A signed leadership policy: no duty outweighs employee health and safety, accidents are preventable, management provides the resources, and every employee holds stop-work authority — no one is ever required to perform work they believe is unsafe.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Policy exists, is current, and is signed by IWS leadership — not a template with blanks
  • Crews can state stop-work authority in their own words
  • A stop-work event has actually happened and was praised, not punished
  • Resources requested for safety got funded
2Employee Responsibilities & General Safety Rules▸
The Program Element

Conduct, fitness for duty, housekeeping, and immediate injury reporting — the daily ground rules: clean aisles and exits, clear breakers and extinguishers, drips wiped before they become slips.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Housekeeping walkdown — wet-floor control in a business where floors are always wet
  • Injury reporting practice vs. policy: do minor injuries actually get reported same-day?
  • Drug & alcohol program administration and coverage
  • Egress, extinguisher, and electrical-panel clearance spot checks
3Safety Orientation & Training Matrix▸
The Program Element

New-hire orientation before first assignment, plus a role-based training matrix: confined space, HazCom, LOTO, fall protection, respiratory protection, first aid/CPR, and equipment certifications.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Matrix mapped against what each standard actually requires per role
  • Records current for every operator and field tech — including refresher cycles
  • Comprehension checks with the crew, not just signature pages
  • Contractor and new-acquisition employees folded in, not grandfathered
4Injury & Incident Management — Return to Work▸
The Program Element

Immediate reporting, incident investigation that finds root cause instead of blame, medical case management, transitional/light-duty jobs that keep injured employees healing and productive, and accurate OSHA recordkeeping behind it all.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • OSHA 300/300A accuracy — cross-checked against first-aid logs and comp claims
  • Incident investigation practice — root cause identified, corrective actions tracked to closure
  • Return-to-work program used in practice, with real transitional assignments
  • TRIR / DART / EMR benchmarked against industry peers for Sciens reporting
  • Near-miss capture — the leading indicator most programs skip
5Emergency Action Plan & Response▸
The Program Element

Site-specific EAPs: evacuation routes and muster points, alarm systems, chemical release response, severe weather, medical emergencies, and posted emergency contacts at every facility and field job.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • EAP is site-specific — not one generic plan across five facilities
  • Chlorine/chemical release scenarios drilled, with alarm function tests
  • Confined-space rescue plan walked at real entry points — the rescue cascade killer
  • Field crews carry job-site emergency info for customer locations
6Employee Protections & Records Access▸
The Program Element

Harassment and workplace-violence policies, and employee access to exposure and medical records under 29 CFR 1910.1020 — the quiet compliance corner most audits skip entirely.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Policies distributed and acknowledged across all acquired companies
  • Exposure and medical records retained, retrievable, and access process known
  • Reporting channels crews would actually use
7Vehicle Use, MVR Grading & Fleet Safety▸
The Program Element

Vehicle use policy, motor vehicle record grading criteria for drivers, DOT/FMCSA compliance where it applies, and telematics-based coaching for a mobile service business that lives on the road.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • MVR grading actually enforced at hire and annually — with documented criteria
  • Driver qualification files, hours-of-service, and inspection records complete
  • Hazmat shipping compliance for media and chemical movements
  • Telematics data reviewed and coached — Canon runs this exact scorecard live today
8Safe Work Procedures — Water Treatment Operations▸
The Program Element

The operational heart of the program — procedure-by-procedure safe work rules matched to the risk matrix: confined space entry with atmosphere testing and gas classification (H2S, methane, O2), chemical handling & HazCom, lockout/tagout, machine guarding, PPE by task, electrical/arc flash, fall protection, cranes & rigging, hot work, compressed gas, pathogen hygiene, and heat stress.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Each procedure audited on the floor with the crew that performs it — Tier 1 risks at every site, every time
  • Gas detection: monitor calibration, bump testing, and alarm response — Canon's home turf
  • Condition-flag system for gas hazard areas adapted from proven field practice
  • Procedures written for water treatment reality, not copied from another industry's template
9Acknowledgement, Enforcement & Continuous Improvement▸
The Program Element

Signed employee acknowledgement of the program, consistent enforcement, and the feedback loop: observations, near-misses, and audit findings flowing back into revised procedures and weekly topics.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Acknowledgements current for every employee — including acquisitions
  • Discipline applied consistently, supervisors included
  • Contractor selection, orientation, and oversight — one standard for employees and contractors alike
  • A revision history that proves the program is alive, not shelf-ware
  • The culture engine (Phase 3) wired to feed this loop permanently
10Environmental Stewardship — Permits, Water & Waste▸
The Program Element

An environmental chapter run with the same discipline as safety: a permit register per site (air, wastewater discharge, stormwater, drinking water), hazardous waste and chemical management, spill prevention and containment, a regulatory reporting calendar — DMRs, Tier II, waste manifests — and operational controls so compliance never depends on one person's memory. Evaluated as a co-equal pillar alongside health & safety and management systems.

What Canon Verifies On Site
  • Permit inventory current per site — expirations, conditions, enforcement history and open agency actions
  • Discharge & stormwater practice: sampling actually performed, DMRs submitted, exceedances investigated
  • Drinking-water obligations verified where IWS operates or commissions potable systems
  • Hazardous waste: determinations documented, accumulation areas compliant, manifests closed out
  • SPCC & secondary containment walked in the field — integrity, inspections, spill kits at transfer points
  • Tier II / EPCRA reporting matches the chemical inventory on the floor
  • Crews know the environmental duties of their own role — awareness audited like safety
